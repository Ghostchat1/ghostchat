Security

How GhostChat
keeps you invisible

A plain-language tour of what protects you, and an honest list of what can't.

Hybrid post-quantum key exchange

Each conversation starts with ML-KEM-768 (the NIST post-quantum standard) combined with P-384 elliptic-curve Diffie-Hellman. An attacker would have to break both.

Double ratchet

Like Signal, keys move forward with every message and old keys are destroyed, giving forward secrecy and recovery after compromise.

Sealed sender

The outer envelope is encrypted to the recipient with a fresh one-time key. The server sees only the destination, never the sender.

AES-256-GCM everywhere

Messages, files, voice notes and your on-device vault are encrypted with authenticated AES-256.

Memory-only server

Our relay keeps nothing on disk. Undelivered envelopes are deleted after 7 days; delivered ones immediately.

Traffic padding & cover noise

Messages are padded to fixed sizes and your app sends random dummy traffic, so activity is hard to infer.

Signed, rotating prekeys

Prekeys are signed by your identity key and rotate daily, so a server can't swap in its own keys unnoticed. Compare safety numbers to be sure.

Tor onion service

Connect through Tor and no one, not your ISP and not us, learns your IP address.

Tested & public

Our code is public on GitHub and has been through our own OWASP-style security testing (VAPT). An independent audit is planned.

Honest limits

No tool makes you invisible on its own. GhostChat can't protect you if:

  • Your device is compromised. Spyware on your phone can read the screen. Keep your phone updated and locked.
  • Someone knows your passcode. Use a few random words, and set a duress passcode for emergencies.
  • The other person shares your messages. They can photograph their screen. Use disappearing messages and view-once photos.
  • You use the normal web address and need to hide your IP. The fast address shows your IP to our hosting provider. For full anonymity use the Tor address.
  • You use the web version. The web app is downloaded from our server each time; the Android app bundles its code instead. High-risk users should prefer the app.
